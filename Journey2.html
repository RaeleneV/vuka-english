Journey 02

Keep More of What You Earn

"You worked hard for every rand today. So where did half of it go by the time you got home?" This journey is about understanding your numbers, cutting the costs you don't need, keeping proof of what you earn, and getting that money somewhere safer than your pocket.

Know Your Three Numbers

"If someone asked what your business actually made last month, could you answer with a number, or just a guess?"

Every trader needs to know three numbers, turnover, expenses and profit. Turnover is all the money that comes in from sales, before any costs are taken out. Expenses are everything you spend: stock, transport, packaging, permit fees, data and airtime. Profit is turnover minus expenses, and it's the only number that tells you what you actually made.

💡 QUICK TIP: At the end of each week, add up all your "Money In" and "Money Out," then subtract. That's your weekly profit, in under five minutes.

Don't Mix Business and Personal Money

"If your business money and your taxi fare money live in the same pocket, how would you ever know which one you're spending?"

Many traders lose track of profit because business and personal cash get mixed together in one pocket or purse. To avoid this, keep two or three separate containers or accounts: one for the business, one for personal or household spending, and ideally one for emergencies. Try paying yourself a fixed amount, like an employee's wage, instead of just taking money out whenever you need it. Set aside 10% to 20% of your weekly profit into a savings container, stokvel, or account too, so you've got a buffer for slower weeks.

💡 QUICK TIP: Treat your own "salary" as a fixed weekly expense, just like stock or rent. It protects your profit from disappearing invisibly.

Stop the Leaks

"You didn't have a bad sales week. So why did you end up with less profit than last month?"

Many small businesses lose money not because they don't sell enough, but because their costs are too high or badly managed. Compare prices between at least two suppliers before committing to one regularly. Reduce waste, especially with fresh food, by only buying what you can realistically sell before it spoils. Avoid mashonisas and unregistered lenders altogether: their interest rates can turn a R1,000 loan into R2,000 or more owed. Every month, look back through your notebook and ask whether there's a cheaper, equally good option for your biggest cost items.

💡 QUICK TIP: Loyalty to one supplier is good, but not if it's quietly costing you money every single week. It's worth the awkward conversation to ask around.

Keep Proof of Every Rand

"A funder asks to see your trading history. What would you actually show them right now?"

Nationally, most unregistered South African businesses keep no financial records at all. Without records, you can't prove your income to a bank, can't apply for funding, and can't really tell if your business is making money. The fix is simple: one notebook, divided into "Money In" and "Money Out." Write every transaction down on the day it happens, even small ones. Keep every receipt from suppliers or the municipality in one plastic sleeve or file, sorted by month. Total everything up weekly and monthly, this becomes your simple financial record and your proof of income when you're ready to apply for support.

💡 QUICK TIP: If you have a smartphone, a free app like Yoco or a simple notes app can back up these records digitally too, on top of your notebook.

Bank It, Even Without CIPC

"All your profit is sitting in a plastic bag under your bed. What happens if it goes missing tomorrow?"

You do not need to register with CIPC to open a business bank account as a sole proprietor. Several South African banks offer accounts built specifically for informal, unregistered traders. The Capitec Entrepreneur Account works off an existing Capitec GlobalOne personal account and your South African ID, with no minimum deposit to open. The TymeBank Everyday Business Account works from an existing TymeBank "Good Friends" personal account, is free to open, and is set up in the app under My Profile > My Details > Business Benefits. Depositing your cash regularly, even weekly, builds a transaction history, which is exactly what you'll need later for funding or a card machine.

💡 QUICK TIP: Keeping cash on your person or at home is one of the biggest theft risks a trader faces. Even a small regular deposit reduces that risk.
