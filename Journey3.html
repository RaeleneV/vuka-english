Journey 03

Make Your Business Official

"Imagine Pick n Pay wants to buy your amagwinya tomorrow. Are you ready?" Most big buyers, funders, and banks won't work with you until your business is official. This journey walks you through exactly what "official" means, one small step at a time. No lectures, just what to do next.

Why Bother Registering?

"You're already selling 100 plates a week from your yard. Why would you need any paperwork at all?"

Right now, you and your business are the same thing in the eyes of the law. If something goes wrong, it's your personal problem, not just "the business's" problem. Once you register, your business becomes its own thing, separate from you (Companies Act 71 of 2008). That's what opens the door to bank accounts, big contracts, and funding.

💡 QUICK TIP You don't have to register everything at once. Start with the one piece of paper that unlocks the most doors: your CIPC registration (next lesson).

Let's Make It Official

"Someone asks: "Is your business registered?" What do you actually say?"

CIPC (the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission) is where every South African business gets its legal ID (Companies Act 71 of 2008). You register online at cipc.co.za. Once you're registered, your business has its own name and its own legal identity, separate from you personally.

💡 QUICK TIP Keep your CIPC certificate saved as a photo on your phone. You'll need to show it again and again, for permits, for the bank, and for funders.

Get Your Food Permit Sorted

"Could a health inspector actually shut you down tomorrow?"

Yes, if you don't have a Certificate of Acceptability (called a CoA), you are trading illegally, even if your food is amazing. This is a permit from your local municipality that proves your kitchen is safe. It's a legal requirement under Regulation R638 (R638 of 2018).

💡 QUICK TIP Your CoA is tied to YOU and YOUR exact spot. If you move stalls or change hands, you need a brand new one. It doesn't transfer.

The Other Permits People Forget

"You've got your CoA. Are you actually fully legal now?"

Not quite. Most food businesses also need a separate Business Licence from the municipality, under the Businesses Act (Businesses Act 71 of 1991). If you cook from home, you may also need permission for your property to be used for business (called zoning). Using gas, or planning to sell alcohol? Add a Gas Certificate and a Liquor Licence to your list too.

💡 QUICK TIP Ask your municipality for a full permit checklist on your first visit. Most people find out about a missing permit one at a time, the hard way.

Sorting Out Your Tax

"If your business made R50,000 last month, do you owe SARS anything?"

It depends on how much your business earns in a year. As of April 2026, you only have to register for VAT once you earn more than R2.3 million a year (SARS, Budget 2026 FAQs). If you earn less, a simpler system called Turnover Tax might work better for you, since it replaces several taxes with one easy calculation.

💡 QUICK TIP Don't guess your number. Check sars.gov.za or visit a SARS branch to see exactly where your business sits, since these thresholds get updated over time.

From Side Hustle to Official Business

"You sell from a stall, a yard, or your kitchen table right now. How do you actually get to be fully legal?"

You don't have to do it all in one week. Requirements depend on where you trade, what you sell, and how your business is set up, so there isn't one fixed order that works for everyone. In general, most people start by checking their spot is zoned or permitted correctly, then register with CIPC, then apply for any licences or permits their trade needs (inspectors often check several at once), then sort out tax once they're properly trading. Since 2024, even spaza shops have had to register, so the government is watching this space more closely than before.

💡 QUICK TIP Pick ONE step to finish this week. Don't try to do everything at once.
