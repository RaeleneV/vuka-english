Journey 04

Customers Should Trust Your Food

"Would you eat in your own kitchen right now?" Trust is the real product you're selling. The food is just how customers experience it. This journey shows you how to earn that trust, and prove it when someone asks.

Would You Eat In Your Own Kitchen?

"Be honest. Would you eat in your own kitchen right now?"

A health inspector checks a few basic things: is your prep area clean and easy to wipe down? Are your fridges cold enough? Do you keep raw meat away from cooked food? Is there somewhere for staff to wash their hands? These are the exact things Regulation R638 (R638 of 2018) checks before giving you a Food Permit.

💡 QUICK TIP: Do your own mini-inspection once a week using this same list, before an inspector ever does it for you.

Five Things Customers Want to Know

"Would a stranger trust your food from how it's packaged, before they even taste it?"

Customers judge food safety before they even eat, from your packaging, your labels, and how you handle food in front of them. If you sell pre-packaged food (not made-to-order), the law (R146) says your label needs a few key things: what the product is, the ingredients, how much is in the pack, and who made it. If it contains a common allergen, that has to be clearly stated too.

💡 QUICK TIP: If you ever write something like "low sugar" or "high protein" on a label, the law (R146) requires a full nutrition table too, so only make that claim if you're ready to back it up.

Keep Food Fresh

"How long can that chicken really sit out before it's a problem?"

Temperature is the single biggest food safety risk. Cold food needs to stay cold, hot food needs to stay hot, and the zone in between is where bacteria grows fastest. This is one of the main things inspectors check under R638 (R638 of 2018).

💡 QUICK TIP: Get a cheap fridge thermometer. Guessing the temperature is how most food businesses fail an inspection.

Simple Cleaning Habits That Keep You Legal

"If an inspector walked in right now, could your staff explain how they were trained?"

The law (R638 of 2018) says the person in charge of your kitchen must have proper food safety training, and so must anyone helping you handle food. You also need to keep some proof of this, even just a simple written record of who was trained and when.

💡 QUICK TIP: Keep training proof as photos or notes on your phone. A simple record beats no record, every time.

Growing? New Rules Might Kick In

"What happens the moment you start canning your famous chilli sauce to sell in stores?"

Some products carry extra risk once you scale. Canned, smoked, or vacuum-packed foods are checked by a separate regulator (the NRCS), not just your municipality. If you want to supply a retailer like Pick n Pay or Checkers, they'll often ask for their own proof of food safety, on top of your permit.

💡 QUICK TIP: Before you invest in new equipment for a new product type, check whether it changes your legal category. It can save you from an expensive mistake.
