Module 05

Never Run Out of Stock Again

"Running out of a popular item halfway through the day sends that customer straight to a competitor. Ordering too much carries the same risk in reverse." Most small South African food businesses still order stock based on guesswork. Below is a simple system that needs no software or technical expertise, just a bit of daily discipline.

First In, First Out

"Yesterday's chicken is still in the back of the fridge. Which batch are you actually cooking with today?"

Know your "first in, first out" rule (FIFO). Always use or sell your oldest stock first, and put new deliveries behind existing stock, not in front of it. This is especially important for perishable food like meat, dairy, and vegetables.

💡 QUICK TIP Make FIFO physical: literally move new stock to the back of the shelf or fridge every time a delivery arrives.

Set Your Par Levels

"How many kilograms of mealie meal do you need left before you start to worry?"

Set a simple "par level" for your top and best-selling items. Decide, from experience, on the minimum amount of each item you should have before you need to reorder, for example: "if I have less than 5kg of mealie meal left, I need to restock before my next trading day."

💡 QUICK TIP Your par level doesn't need to be scientific. Your own experience of what "running low" feels like is a perfectly good starting point.

notebook routine. Write down what you sold out of, and when. If you notice you're consistently selling out of an item by midday, that's your sign to increase your order for that item.

Order Smart, Not by Guesswork

"Your veggies spoil before you sell them, but you never run out of packaging. What does that tell you about how you're ordering?"

Match your ordering to your trading pattern. If certain items frequently get spoiled, order in smaller amounts more frequently for those (fresh vegetables, meat), while ordering slightly larger amounts less often for things that last longer (dry goods, packaging, disposables). Build reliable supplier relationships too, a supplier who knows you will often prioritise you when stock is tight, or give you advance warning of price changes.

💡 QUICK TIP A five-minute conversation with your supplier about your usual order can save you an unplanned trip, or an unplanned shortage.

✅ QUICK CHALLENGE
