Journey 06

Let Your Phone Bring You Customers

"Your food sells itself once people taste it. But how do they find you before that?" Almost everyone in South Africa already uses WhatsApp, and it's free to use for business, only data is needed, which is cheaper than airtime. Very few food sellers set it up the right way. Done properly, it makes your business look professional and lets regular customers order ahead or check what's on the menu today.

Set Up WhatsApp Business

"Someone wants to order 10 plates for a stokvel meeting. Can they message you, or do they have to physically find your stall first?"

Download "WhatsApp Business" (not regular WhatsApp) from the Play Store or App Store, it's free. Register with a phone number, either a second SIM if you want to keep business and personal chats separate, or your existing number if it isn't already linked to regular WhatsApp. Verify the number by SMS code, then set up your Business Profile: business name, category ("Food & Beverage"), a short description of what you sell, your trading location and hours, and a clear profile photo of your food, stall, or logo.

💡 QUICK TIP: Set a greeting message (e.g. "Thanks for messaging Thandi's Kota Corner! We're open Mon–Fri, 6am–17:30 at the taxi rank.") so customers get an instant response, even while you're busy serving.

Build a Catalogue Customers Can Browse

"A regular customer wants to know what's on the menu today, without calling you. What do they actually see?"

Under the Tools/Business Tools menu, select "Catalogue" and add your items, a photo, name, and price for each. Share your WhatsApp number or link with customers by writing it on a small sign or your menu board at your stall, so people can save it.

💡 QUICK TIP: Keep your catalogue updated when items sell out or prices change. An outdated catalogue creates awkward conversations, not sales.

Connect Facebook and Instagram

"Someone finds your food on Instagram. Can they message you straight from there, or do they have to go searching for your number separately?"

Facebook and Instagram can find your next customer, and you can connect them from the WhatsApp Business app itself. Open the app and go to Tools, tap Facebook & Instagram, then tap through to connect whichever platform you use. Log in to your Facebook or Instagram account, select the Page you want to link, and tap Connect WhatsApp.

💡 QUICK TIP: You don't need both platforms at once. Start with whichever one your customers already use most.

Take Photos People Want to Eat

"Would a photo of your food make a stranger hungry, or would they scroll straight past it?"

A photo of your food often decides whether a customer wants to try your dish or not. The picture sets the mood and makes people feel hungry before they've even tasted anything. That's why it matters for customers to see pictures of your food, whether on your menu or your WhatsApp catalogue. Good food photos also get people talking, sharing, commenting, and tagging friends. Posts with images get far more views than posts with text alone (candyfoxstudio.com), so an appetising photo can create more interest than any description ever could.

💡 QUICK TIP: Natural light does more for a food photo than any filter. Shoot near a window or outside, not under a dim stall light.

Never Run Out of Things to Post

"It's been two weeks since your last post. What's actually stopping you?"

To keep your social media active without running out of ideas, share everyday moments from your business: preparing food, answering common customer questions, showing off your most popular menu items, or celebrating milestones. Keep an "idea bank" on your phone, a running list of trends, funny videos, and memorable pictures, so you always have content ready to post.

💡 QUICK TIP: You don't need a big production. A quick, honest photo of your actual stall usually beats a polished but generic one.
