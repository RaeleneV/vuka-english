Journey 07

Make People Remember You

"Two stalls sell the same kota, at the same price, in the same street. Why does one have a queue, and the other doesn't?" Branding is how people see and remember your food business. It's built through every item of food you sell, and every interaction you have with customers, every single day.

What Branding Actually Means (And Why Bother)

""Let me go buy amagwinya from that lady" or "let me go buy amagwinya from [your business name]": which one turns a one-time buyer into a regular?"

More than 80% of people say trust is a leading reason why they buy, which is why branding matters no matter how big or small your food business is (Cloudfusion, 2025). Good branding helps people remember you, and people don't forget a business with the same colours and packaging every time (Dandu, 2015). It also builds trust: when customers see the same neat packaging and name every time, they feel emotionally safe buying from you, and with so many other people selling food, being memorable is what makes people choose you over someone else. Branding matters for your team too. It makes employees feel proud to be part of something real and growing, not just doing a small job (Cloudfusion, 2025).

💡 QUICK TIP Branding isn't a logo you design once and forget. It's the sum of every small, repeated detail a customer experiences.

Know Who You're Really Talking To

"If your branding tried to please absolutely everyone, would anyone actually remember it?"

Start by understanding who your audience is: the specific group of people your food business serves, and the problem you're solving for them. Your branding should connect with their habits, likes, dislikes, and daily life. From there, build a brand strategy, a plan for your long-term goals and how your business will meet them. Two of its core elements are brand purpose (why does your business exist, and what value does it add to your audience's life? This becomes your mission statement) and brand vision (where do you see your business in the future? This shapes your long-term goals) (Yolisa Motha, 2021).

💡 QUICK TIP If you're not sure who your audience is, look at who's already buying from you regularly, and build outward from there.

Give Your Brand a Voice and a Vibe

"If your business could talk, would it crack a joke, or keep things strictly formal?"

Your brand also needs a personality, to make it more human and relatable. Think about how you want customers to see you: personable or corporate, exciting or calm, modern or rooted in tradition, playful or serious, accessible or premium. From that personality comes your brand voice, how your brand speaks and its tone, as if it were a real person talking to a customer. Finally, your brand tagline is a short, memorable line that sums all of this up in one breath (Yolisa Motha, 2021).

💡 QUICK TIP Say your tagline out loud to a friend or family member. If they can repeat it back a minute later, it's working.

Build a Look People Recognise

"Could a customer spot your packaging in a crowd of ten other food stalls, from across the taxi rank?"

Beyond strategy, you need a brand identity, the visual elements that make up your brand. Your logo is the face of your business, the symbol people recognise before they even read your name; a good logo is simple, memorable, and affordable (some of the world's biggest logos cost only a few hundred rand to design). Even shapes carry meaning: round shapes feel warm and inviting, squares and rectangles feel strong and reliable, vertical lines feel bold, horizontal lines feel calm. Your colour palette matters too, since colours trigger feelings: red creates an urge to eat, green suggests fresh and healthy ingredients. Start with one to three main colours, then build outward. Keep your typography, the fonts you use, consistent everywhere your business name appears, from your menu to your social media (Yolisa Motha, 2021).

💡 QUICK TIP Consistency beats perfection. The same colours and fonts used everywhere, even if simple, are more powerful than an elaborate design used inconsistently.
