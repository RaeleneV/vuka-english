Journey 08

Work Smarter

"You're already working hard. But is your day actually working for you, or are you just reacting to whatever happens next?" This journey pulls together the everyday habits, communication, time, teamwork, professionalism, your network, and how you get paid, that separate a business that runs smoothly from one that just runs.

Talk to Customers and Suppliers the Right Way

"A customer says yesterday's food was cold. Do you jump in to explain, or do you actually let them finish first?"

How well you communicate is directly linked to how well your business performs. Good communication builds trust, and trust is what brings a customer back. Greet every customer, a simple "Sawubona," "Dumela," or "Good morning" before talking about price or product sets a respectful tone. Listen before you speak: if a customer complains, let them finish before you respond, this is called active listening, and it's one of the most important skills a small business owner can have. Be clear about price and product every time, don't wait for a customer to ask twice. And keep the same polite, clear approach with suppliers: always confirm what you're ordering, how much it is, and when you'll pay or collect.

💡 QUICK TIP: Practise the hard conversations before they happen. Run through a customer complaint or a late-delivery call with a family member so it feels familiar in the moment.

Structure Your Day So It Doesn't Run You

"Where did your morning actually go? Could you account for every hour of it?"

It isn't a lack of product knowledge that holds most South African traders back, it's time management. On average, small business owners lose close to 96 minutes a day to disorganised or wasted time. A simple daily structure helps: before you leave home, prepare stock, count change, and check your equipment. Arrive early enough to set up before the busiest hour. During trading hours, focus only on selling and serving, don't do admin in the middle of a rush. Use quiet periods to restock, clean, or update your notebook. At the end of the day, count cash, write down what you sold and spent, and plan tomorrow's stock.

💡 QUICK TIP: Set a phone alarm for "restock time" and "packing up time," so the day doesn't quietly run away from you.

Build a Team, Even a Small One

"If you got sick tomorrow, would the person helping you know exactly what to do?"

Even a one- or two-person stall benefits from clear roles. Owners with reliable help, even informal help from family, can spend more time on things that actually grow the business, like sourcing better stock or scouting new selling spots. If you work with even one other person, agree out loud, don't just assume, on who cooks, who serves, and who handles the money. Have one clear "money person" at any given time, to avoid confusion and mistrust. Say thank you and acknowledge good work, it costs nothing and keeps helpers motivated. If you ever expand beyond one or two helpers, agree on simple, fair rules in advance: start and stop times, how pay or profit is split, and what happens if someone doesn't show up.

💡 QUICK TIP: A written agreement doesn't need to be formal. A few lines in your notebook is enough to prevent most disputes before they start.

Act Like the Pro You Already Are

"Would a municipal official, a supplier, and a first-time customer all take your business seriously today?"

Around 70% of small businesses in South Africa fail within their first year, often due to a lack of consistency and professional practice, not bad products. Professionalism is what makes people take you seriously even without a fancy shop. Sell from the same spot, at the same times, as much as possible, customers return to what they can rely on. Keep your hands, containers, and stall clean, which is also a legal hygiene requirement. Keep your pricing fair and consistent from customer to customer. If something goes wrong, apologise and offer a fair solution calmly, rather than arguing in public. And keep your word: if you tell a supplier or customer a time, price, or quantity, honour it. Reliability is your reputation.

Build a Network That Backs You Up

"Who would warn you if an inspector was heading your way this afternoon?"

Traders with wider networks, other traders, suppliers, community contacts, get better access to information, funding, and support during hard times than those working in isolation. This is especially true at taxi ranks, where many vendors trade side by side. Get to know the traders around you: they're not only competitors, they can share supplier contacts, warn you about inspections, watch your stall, or refer overflow customers your way. Join or form a trader group, many taxi ranks and townships already have informal associations; your municipality's Economic Development office can usually tell you if one exists nearby. Keep supplier contacts saved on your phone with notes on pricing and reliability, and attend free local events run by SEDA or your municipality when you can. WhatsApp groups are a free, low-effort way to stay connected with everyone at once.

💡 QUICK TIP: Your network is also your early-warning system. A quick WhatsApp message from a neighbouring trader can save you a fine, or a wasted trip.

Accept Card Payments with Yoco

"A customer wants to tap their card, but you only take cash. Do they buy anyway, or walk to the next stall?"

Nearly 6 million small South African businesses still only accept cash, but many customers now carry less of it, worried about theft. Accepting both cash and card gives you the best of both worlds, and you do not need to be CIPC-registered to sign up with Yoco. Go to yoco.com or download the Yoco app, select "Get Started," and choose Sole Proprietor. Enter your personal details and verify your identity with a photo of your South African ID. Add your trading address (your home address is fine if you trade from there) and a South African bank account in your own name. Choose your card machine, an entry-level Yoco Go costs roughly R800 upfront with a small daily fee only on days you use it, and no monthly fee. Once it arrives, it's already linked to your profile.

💡 QUICK TIP: Card payments land in your account the next business day, not instantly, so plan your cash flow around that if you need cash on hand to buy stock.
